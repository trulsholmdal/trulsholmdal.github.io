01 / RESEARCH QUESTION

Bringing modern methods to a classic macroeconomic problem.

Forecasting inflation is central to monetary policy and economic decision-making. This project examines whether a broad set of macroeconomic predictors, combined with regularised regression and machine learning, can improve on parsimonious benchmarks when predicting Norwegian inflation.

The proposed study asks how model accuracy differs across forecast horizons, which economic variables are selected by LASSO, and whether relative predictive performance changed after the rise in inflation from 2021.

Research integrity

This page describes the research design and intended evaluation. It does not report empirical model rankings, forecast errors or findings until the analysis is complete.

02 / FORECAST DESIGN

Four horizons. A consistent information set.

The target is the 12-month log change in the Consumer Price Index (CPI). Direct forecasts are constructed separately for each horizon, using only information available at the forecast date. CPI adjusted for tax changes and excluding energy products (CPI-ATE) is planned as a robustness check.

03 / DATA & METHODS

From public datasets to comparable forecasts.

The proposed monthly dataset draws from Statistics Norway, Norges Bank, FRED and Eurostat, covering inflation, interest rates, labour markets, exchange rates, activity, credit and international prices.

04 / EVALUATION FRAMEWORK

Out-of-sample performance matters.

The proposed assessment uses an expanding training window, beginning with 2006–2014 and producing forecasts from January 2015 onward. Hyperparameter selection and standardisation are designed to use training-period information only.

Planned measures include root mean squared error (RMSE), mean absolute error (MAE), model comparisons relative to the autoregressive benchmark and Diebold–Mariano forecast comparison tests. Subperiod comparisons separate 2015–2020 from 2021 onward.

Illustrative research pipeline

Public APIs → monthly alignment and publication lags → expanding-window estimation → model forecasts → error comparison and predictor interpretation.
